# The Foundry — Brand Guide

Every piece of copy, every ad, every caption gets run through one filter: **would Elena stop scrolling for this?**

---

## The Persona: Elena Stone

**Who she is:** Elena is a professional, creative woman in Onondaga County, 25–35, planning a wedding that actually feels like *her*. She's not looking for generic — she's curating an experience that tells a story, from the invitations down to the lighting cues. She's organized, sentimental, detail-obsessed, and she has good taste and knows it.

**Location:** Within a 50-mile radius of Syracuse, NY.

**Tone of voice:** Sophisticated, confident, aspirational — with a trendy edge that speaks to a 20–30-year-old sensibility.

**Values:**
- Modern romance
- Clean aesthetics
- Personalization
- Flawless execution

**Sound & mood references:** Cinematic pop, emotional instrumental. Favorite song: *"Golden Hour"* by JVKE — think warm, glowing, a little cinematic, a moment you'd want to freeze.

**Why she works as our north star:** Elena *is* the buyer. She represents the core audience The Foundry is trying to reach — someone who wants a venue that gets out of the way of the story she's telling, and a marketing voice that respects her intelligence and her taste at the same time.

---

## Voice principles

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Write like you're speaking to a smart friend with great taste | Write like a wedding vendor blasting a mass email |
| Lead with feeling, follow with the practical detail | Bury the emotional hook under logistics |
| Be confident — state what The Foundry offers plainly | Oversell, use excessive exclamation points, or beg |
| Use clean, specific, sensory language | Use generic wedding-industry clichés ("your special day," "dream come true") |
| Let urgency feel like scarcity of a good thing, not pressure | Use countdown-clock panic tactics |

**Established sweet spot:** *Confident but warm.* Aspirational without feeling untouchable; urgent without feeling pushy. If a draft reads more "luxury real estate listing" than "friend giving you the inside scoop," pull it back toward warm.

---

## Copy checklist

Before anything ships, ask:

1. Would Elena feel *seen* by this, or marketed at?
2. Does it sound sophisticated without sounding stiff?
3. Is the personalization angle present — her wedding, her story — not just The Foundry's features?
4. Is the aesthetic language clean (no clutter, no jargon)?
5. Does the urgency (deal deadlines, booking windows, event dates) feel like a nudge, not a shove?

---

## Visual direction

- Clean, uncluttered compositions — negative space is a feature, not a gap to fill
- Warm, golden-hour lighting where possible (on-brand with the Elena persona's favorite song)
- Personalization details in focus: place settings, handwriting, small custom touches — not just wide venue shots
- Consistent color/type treatment across Canva and Higgsfield exports (see `/assets/`)

## Messaging pillars by campaign type

- **Winter wedding deal ($5,000 package):** Frame as a smart, sophisticated choice — not a discount bin. Elena books value, not clearance.
- **2027–2028 long-lead booking:** Speak to the planner in her — the peace of mind of locking in early, the story of watching a date come together over time.
- **Fall Fest (9/26/26) / Holiday Market (12/6/26):** These are lower-commitment, high-charm touchpoints — invite her in to experience the space before she's ready to book.
